## Worth reading today

**Ambarella runs SmolVLA, SLAM and navigation on one chip**

Ambarella says its N1-655 ran SmolVLA, SLAM, navigation and pick-and-place in a mobile-manipulator demo. It also introduced the X7 standalone accelerator. The demo offers a workload-integration signal; sustained performance, power and customer production evidence were not disclosed. [Source](https://www.ambarella.com/blog/ambarella-at-the-ai-infrastructure-summit-in-santa-clara)

**PReFlow reports progress in offline-to-online policy learning**

The paper’s abstract describes critic-based proposal selection and a conditional refinement flow, with results on OGBench. It offers a lead in policy learning; real-robot deployment and SoC measurements were not disclosed. [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36812)

**Boston Dynamics drops the little finger from its new hand; the trade-offs still need verification**

The report’s headline says the new humanoid hand drops the little finger; its short summary describes engineers trying tasks with their little and ring fingers tied together. The design trade-off is worth following, but this thin evidence cannot establish hand specifications or performance. [Source](https://www.therobotreport.com/boston-dynamics-drops-pinkie-on-new-humanoid-hand)

## Watching next

- Look for latency, power, memory/bandwidth and independent or customer validation under the same configuration; a demo is not production deployment.
- Follow real-robot validation, implementation details and end-to-end runtime measurements; research benchmarks do not establish deployment performance.
- Obtain more accessible evidence on the hand design before judging degrees of freedom, grasping ability or the specific trade-offs; do not infer performance from the current summary.
