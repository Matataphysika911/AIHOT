These three verified samples bring this week’s attention beyond the model: workload coordination on silicon, the path from policy learning to deployment, and trade-offs in hand design. ## Compute questions are becoming questions about workloads running together

Ambarella says its N1-655 ran SmolVLA, SLAM, navigation and pick-and-place in a mobile-manipulator demo. It also introduced the X7 standalone accelerator. The demo offers a workload-integration signal; sustained performance, power and customer production evidence were not disclosed.

The demo places the model, perception and navigation in the same configuration. The next question is how they share resources. Without sustained measurements or customer production data, an integration demo cannot be treated as a mature product.

[Source](https://www.ambarella.com/blog/ambarella-at-the-ai-infrastructure-summit-in-santa-clara)

## Policy learning advances, while deployment remains a separate test

The paper’s abstract describes critic-based proposal selection and a conditional refinement flow, with results on OGBench. It offers a lead in policy learning; real-robot deployment and SoC measurements were not disclosed.

Algorithm benchmarks and real robots face different constraints. Read alongside the chip demo, this raises a shared verification question: how can a learned capability remain usable in a concrete system? It is still a question, not evidence that the two announcements form an established trend.

[Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36812)

## Hand design brings manipulation trade-offs into view, with thin evidence

The report’s headline says the new humanoid hand drops the little finger; its short summary describes engineers trying tasks with their little and ring fingers tied together. The design trade-off is worth following, but this thin evidence cannot establish hand specifications or performance.

The available headline and short summary justify attention to design trade-offs, but do not support comparisons of degrees of freedom or grasping performance. They add a mechanical perspective to the week’s observations without proving a shift across the humanoid industry.

[Source](https://www.therobotreport.com/boston-dynamics-drops-pinkie-on-new-humanoid-hand)

## What to watch next week

- Look for latency, power, memory/bandwidth and independent or customer validation under the same configuration; a demo is not production deployment.
- Follow real-robot validation, implementation details and end-to-end runtime measurements; research benchmarks do not establish deployment performance.
- Obtain more accessible evidence on the hand design before judging degrees of freedom, grasping ability or the specific trade-offs; do not infer performance from the current summary.
